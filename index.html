What is a Favicon?

A favicon (short for favorite icon) is the icon that appears on a browser tab, next to a website’s title. Even though it looks small, a favicon plays a big role in making a website recognizable and professional.

Favicon Matters Because of:


Identity – Makes You stand out/be easily identifiable.
Professional Look – Makes your site look complete and trustworthy.
User Experience – Creates consistency across browsers, bookmarks, and search results.
To create a favicon, design a simple icon using tools like Photoshop, Canva, or Favicon.io, keeping it clear since favicons are usually 16x16 or 32x32 pixels. Save it in the right format (.ico, .png, or .svg), upload it to your website, and connect it with HTML code: <link rel="icon" type="image/png" href="favicon.png">.

What is a Selection Tool?

A selection tool is used in image editing and design software to highlight, isolate, or manipulate specific parts of an image. With selection tools, you can move, copy, adjust, or transform selected areas without affecting the rest of the image.

Selection Tools Matter Because of:


Precision – Allows you to target only the areas you want to edit.
Efficiency – Saves time by avoiding unnecessary changes.
Creativity – Opens up possibilities for combining, cutting, or blending parts of images.
Control – Gives flexibility in editing without damaging the whole image.

Types of Selection Tools



Move Tool – Used to reposition a selected area, object, or an entire layer.


Rectangular Marquee Tool – Makes rectangular or square selections in an image.


Photoshop Lasso Tool – A freehand selection tool used to draw around objects or areas in an image.


Magic Wand Tool – Selects areas of similar color with just one click.


Quick Selection Tool – Works like a brush that automatically detects and selects similar colors, textures, and edges as you drag.


Inverse Selection – Flips your current selection, selecting everything outside of it instead.


Feathering Selection – Softens the edges of a selection by creating a gradual transition between the selected area and the background.
